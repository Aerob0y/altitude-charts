A small population can host a big visitor market

Where people live and where visitors stay are two very different maps of New Zealand.

Auckland has 32.9% of the residents in this comparison and 22.5% of international commercial guest nights. Queenstown has just 0.6% of residents, but 17.3% of those nights.

That is a lot of visitors for a small local population. It helps explain why a town can need services and transport on a scale its resident numbers alone would never suggest.

I find this a useful starting point for thinking about airports. Local residents provide one market. People travelling to the region provide another.

The maps do not measure airport passengers. Visitors can arrive through another airport, drive between regions or stay outside commercial accommodation.

But the contrast asks a useful question: when we plan for a place, are we counting only the people who live there, or also the people who come to stay?

Source: MBIE Accommodation Data Programme (ADP), international guest nights in commercial accommodation (hotels, motels, holiday parks, backpackers and lodges; not holiday homes or Airbnb; not all visitor stays or unique visitors), all property types, October 2025 to September 2026. Stats NZ estimated resident population by SA2 at 30 June 2025, added up by Stats NZ regional tourism organisation areas 2020.
Units: percentage of the New Zealand total. Population shares use the SA2 total (5,131,370 people) and nights shares use the published ADP New Zealand total (15,087,700 nights). Gap = international nights share minus population share, in percentage points.
Coverage: 144,400 international nights (1.0% of the total) are not attributed to any reported region. Horowhenua, Kawerau, Rangitikei, Tararua, Waimate have no separate ADP figure, so they are hatched and have no gap. 4,890 residents live outside the mapped tourism regions.
Data period: October 2025 to September 2026.
