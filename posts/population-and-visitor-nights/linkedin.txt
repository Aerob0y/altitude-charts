A small population can host a big visitor market

Where people live and where visitors stay are two very different maps of New Zealand.

Auckland has 32.9% of the residents in this comparison and 22.5% of international commercial guest nights. Queenstown has just 0.6% of residents, but 17.3% of those nights.

That is a lot of visitors for a small local population. It helps explain why a town can need services and transport on a scale its resident numbers alone would never suggest.

I find this a useful starting point for thinking about airports. Local residents provide one market. People travelling to the region provide another.

The maps do not measure airport passengers. Visitors can arrive through another airport, drive between regions or stay outside commercial accommodation.

But the contrast asks a useful question: when we plan for a place, are we counting only the people who live there, or also the people who come to stay?

Source: MBIE Accommodation Data Programme (year to September 2026); Stats NZ resident population (30 June 2025).

Maps: https://csv.co.nz/posts/population-and-visitor-nights/
