How complex is what New Zealand sells?

I'd always assumed what we sell overseas is fairly simple: milk powder, logs, meat. Harvard's economic complexity ranking more or less agrees.

The Growth Lab at Harvard University scores each country on how many different goods it exports, and how few other countries can make them. Lots of products that few others can make means a high score.

In 2024 New Zealand ranked 49th out of 145 countries. That puts us behind Ireland (14th), Denmark (22nd) and Norway (40th), but well ahead of Australia (73rd).

We've drifted down a little over time: 40th in 1995, 53rd for most of 2016 to 2021, and 49th now. Australia has fallen much further, from 33rd in 1995 to 73rd, and has ranked below us every year since 2004.

There's a catch, though. The ranking only counts goods. Tourism, education and software don't show up at all, and those are some of the first things I'd mention if someone asked what New Zealand is good at.

So I'd read this as a ranking of what we ship, rather than everything we do. I'd be curious to see where we'd land if services were counted too.

Source: The Growth Lab at Harvard University, Growth Projections and Complexity Rankings, Harvard Dataverse (version 12.0, released 8 February 2026).
Units: Economic Complexity Index score and world rank, yearly, goods exports only (HS92 product classification).
