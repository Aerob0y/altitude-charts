New Zealand’s accommodation recovery depends on where you look

Occupancy has recovered from the disruption of COVID-19, but the national average hides large differences between destinations and types of accommodation.

In September 2021, during the Delta outbreak, the Accommodation Data Programme estimated that 19.6% of available stay units were occupied nationally. In September 2026, the figure was 48.6%.

Not every destination has followed the same path. In September 2026, estimated occupancy was 75.7% in Queenstown, 65.4% in Auckland and 22.9% in Northland. Each area has a different mix of accommodation, seasons and visitors. I would not treat this as a league table of business performance.

Hotels and holiday parks tell different stories

Accommodation type matters. In September 2026, hotels had estimated occupancy of 69.5%, while holiday parks and campgrounds were at 19.4%. Motels and apartments sat between those figures.

A hotel room and a holiday-park site serve different markets, and their busy seasons differ. September is only one month. Occupancy is the share of available stay units that were occupied, so a change in available capacity can affect it as well as a change in demand.

The COVID shock differed by type too. In September 2021, estimated hotel occupancy was 19.7%, compared with 34.8% for smaller motels and apartments. Auckland remained under lockdown restrictions for much of that month, while restrictions elsewhere eased earlier. The Royal Commission’s account sets out the timeline.

International guests matter more in some regions

In the 12 months to September 2026, international guests accounted for around 65% of guest nights in Queenstown, compared with 26% in Wellington and 22% in Northland.

Nationally, the programme estimated 51,300 international guest nights and 869,100 domestic guest nights in September 2021. By September 2026, the estimates were 968,800 and 1.87 million, respectively.

A guest night counts one person staying one night. Two people staying three nights account for six guest nights. Regional tourism organisation areas do not necessarily match council boundaries.

What about before COVID?

The earlier Stats NZ Accommodation Survey reported national occupancy of 39.0% in September 2019. It ended with that month; the replacement programme began in June 2020, leaving an eight-month gap.

MBIE says the surveys use different methods and are not directly comparable. The 2019 figure is useful context, but it cannot establish precisely how much occupancy has changed since before COVID. Property categories also changed between the surveys.

For an airport, tourism organisation or accommodation operator, the national average is a starting point. I want to know where visitors stay, which accommodation they use, and how much demand comes from New Zealand and overseas.
