Is pay keeping up with prices?

A pay rise can look good on paper and still leave you feeling worse off.

In the June 2026 quarter, New Zealand hourly earnings were up 2.9% on a year earlier. Consumer prices rose 4.1%. Pay growth was 1.2 percentage points behind inflation.

That ended 15 consecutive quarters in which average hourly earnings grew faster than prices. The private sector, public sector and labour cost index measures also fell behind inflation in June.

I use this chart to look past the size of a pay rise. Above zero, wage growth is beating inflation. Below zero, prices are winning. It is a national comparison: your own pay and household bills may tell a different story.

For households, the gap helps explain why a higher wage does not always feel like progress. For businesses, it is a reason to watch how much room customers have to spend.

I will be watching whether the next wage figures close that gap.
