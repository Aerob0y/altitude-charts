Are we spending less?

The same number on the till can hide a very different story.

Card spending in New Zealand was NZ$9,683 million in September 2026, seasonally adjusted. Three years earlier it was NZ$9,712 million. In dollars, almost nothing had changed.

Prices had changed, though. Using the consumers price index, that September 2023 spending was worth NZ$10,603 million in September 2026 prices. On this measure, spending fell about 9% over three years.

Even compared with September 2025, spending rose 1.1% in dollars but fell 2.5% after inflation.

The two lines meet at the right-hand end, because I have put everything into September 2026 prices. The gap on the left is inflation. The Covid lockdowns are the big dips.

I find the inflation-adjusted line more useful when asking how much spending has recovered. A business can have steady sales in dollars while its customers have less purchasing power.

What has dropped over 20 years?

I also looked back 20 years, comparing the latest 12 months with the same 12 months in 2006, all in September 2026 prices.

Most categories are up. Hospitality is up about 120% and services about 113%. Fuel is down about 16% and apparel about 10%.

Why is fuel down?

Fuel surprised me, given what petrol costs now, so I checked whether I had the adjustment wrong. I don't think I have.

I use the broad consumers price index for everything. Petrol has risen faster than that. Since September 2019 the petrol price index is up about 43%, against about 32% for all prices. So a fuel-specific adjustment would make fuel look weaker, not stronger.

In dollars, monthly fuel spending was about NZ$588 million in September 2019 and NZ$524 million in September 2026. Dividing by the petrol price index, that suggests roughly 38% less fuel bought, though I would treat that as a rough guide.

I can only guess at the reasons. Cars are more efficient, there are more hybrids and electric cars, and people may drive less. Some fuel may also be paid for with fuel cards or business accounts, which could fall outside this card measure. I haven't been able to test that.

This covers card payments, rather than all household spending, and uses the broad CPI to adjust prices. It also measures the total, rather than spending per person.

I will be watching for growth that gets ahead of prices.
