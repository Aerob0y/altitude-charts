Australia looks to be pulling ahead of New Zealand

I put New Zealand and Australia side by side on five measures. On most of them, Australia seems to be doing a little better.

After inflation, wages in both countries are below where they were in 2019. New Zealand is about 7% below and Australia about 5% below. Both measures follow what employers pay for the same job, so they're a fair match.

Both countries hit about 4.3% wage growth in 2024. Since then New Zealand has slowed to 2.1%, while Australia is still at 3.5%. I suspect that's most of the gap in the first chart.

Unemployment is where it turns around. In late 2022 the two were level at about 3.5%. New Zealand is now at 5.7% and Australia is at 4.6%.

One place New Zealand still leads is participation. About 70% of working-age people here are in work or looking, against about 67% in Australia.

Spending tells a similar story. Since the end of 2023 New Zealand households have spent less each year after inflation, down 1.8% in the latest quarter. Australian households are up 2.7%.

This doesn't say one economy is better run than the other. I'll be watching whether New Zealand's wage growth picks up.

Source: Stats NZ via the Reserve Bank of New Zealand, Australian Bureau of Statistics and OECD.
Units: as shown under each chart. Latest data is the September 2026 quarter.
