We're so (not) back

Travel volumes are approaching 2019 levels, but it's not hitting quite the same.

In the 12 months to August 2026, about 3.08 million New Zealand residents arrived home from overseas, 0.6% below 2019. So we're back to travelling. But 3.71 million overseas visitors arrived in New Zealand, 4.5% below 2019. So the tourists aren't.

The Australians are back, but long-haul is on struggle street. China and Hong Kong are down about 20% and Europe about 16%.

This seems to be explained in part by the stunning success of Queenstown, where visitor arrivals are up 58% on 2019. Australians through Queenstown are up about 55%, around 129,000 more a year. Outside Queenstown, Australian visitor numbers are about 5% below 2019. My guess is that more direct flights are bringing people straight there, and that's coming at the expense of Auckland.

In part there seems to be a reorientation in why we travel. Business, education and conference travel are all down (about 40%, 42% and 12%). Holidays and visiting friends and relatives are neutral, within about 1% of 2019. I suspect some of the business travel has gone to video calls, but that's a guess.

These are trips, not people, so someone who travels twice counts twice. It's one 12-month period against one year, so I'll keep an eye on it as the next few months come in.
