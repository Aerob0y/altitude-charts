Who's back since Covid? Holidaymakers, mostly

Visitor numbers are close to 2019 again, but not every group has come back. Here's who has, and where we're going ourselves.

In the 12 months to August 2026, about 3.71 million overseas visitors arrived in New Zealand. In 2019 it was 3.89 million. So we're about 5% short overall. That's closer than I expected, but the total hides quite a mix.

Australians are back and then some, up about 4% on 2019. Visitors from the Americas are level, with the United States up about 4%. The Pacific Islands and the rest of Asia are only a couple of per cent short. Within Asia, India and Taiwan are both up about a quarter, while Japan and South Korea are still down about a fifth.

The two big gaps are Europe, down about 16%, and China and Hong Kong, down about 20%. Between them that's around 180,000 fewer visitors a year than in 2019.

Holidays are back. Business trips aren't.

The clearest split is by why people come. Holiday visitors and people visiting friends and family are both back at 2019 levels, give or take a per cent. Business visitors are down about 40%, and education visitors by about the same. I suspect some of that business travel has gone for good to video calls, but that's a guess rather than something the data shows.

Where visitors land has changed too. Visitor arrivals at Queenstown Airport are up about 58% on 2019, while Auckland is down about 12% and Christchurch is roughly level. My guess is that more direct flights into Queenstown mean more people skip Auckland altogether.

Kiwis are travelling as much as before, just not to the same places

New Zealanders came home from about 3.08 million overseas trips in the latest 12 months, almost exactly the 3.10 million of 2019. Australia and the Pacific are still our main destinations, and both are a touch up.

The shift is further afield. Trips to Asia (outside China) are up about 24%, with Japan up about 54% and Vietnam up about 45%. Trips to China are up about a fifth. Meanwhile trips to the Americas are down about 29%, and to the United States alone about 36%. Europe is down about 11%. It looks like Kiwis have swapped some long-haul trips for Asia, though I'd want another year of data before calling it a trend.

These are trips, not people, so someone who travels twice counts twice. And this is one 12-month period against one year, so I'll keep an eye on it as the next few months come in.
