A migration gain can hide a loss of New Zealanders

New Zealand gained 22,600 people through migration in the year to September 2026. But I think that single number hides two quite different stories.

There was a net gain of 60,400 non-New Zealand citizens, alongside a net loss of 37,800 New Zealand citizens. Net means arrivals minus departures, so people are moving in both directions. About 26,700 New Zealanders came home to live, and about 64,500 left.

The longer view is the interesting part for me. On these figures, New Zealand has lost more of its own citizens than it gained in every 12-month period since 2001, apart from the Covid years, when many came home. The loss peaked at about 45,900 in the year to April 2024. It's a little smaller now, but not by much.

So a growing population can sit alongside a steady loss of New Zealand citizens. For employers, the useful questions are probably who is arriving, who is leaving, and whether their skills match the jobs available.

Citizenship tells us which passport someone used, not where they came from or where they are going. A New Zealander leaving isn't automatically moving to Australia.

These are provisional estimates of people changing their country of residence, not holiday trips. Stats NZ will revise them as it learns how long travellers actually stay. I'd watch both sides of the flow rather than the total.
