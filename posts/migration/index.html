A migration gain can hide a loss of New Zealanders

New Zealand gained 22,600 people through migration in the year to September 2026. But that single number hides two very different stories.

There was a net gain of 60,400 non-New Zealand citizens, alongside a net loss of 37,800 New Zealand citizens. Net means arrivals minus departures: people are moving in both directions.

That is why I look past the headline. A growing population can sit alongside a loss of New Zealand citizens. For employers, the useful questions are who is arriving, who is leaving, and whether their skills match the jobs available.

Citizenship tells us which passport someone used, rather than where they came from or where they are going. A New Zealander leaving is not automatically moving to Australia.

These are provisional estimates of people changing their country of residence, not holiday trips. They will be revised as Stats NZ learns more about how long travellers stay. I would watch both sides of the flow.
