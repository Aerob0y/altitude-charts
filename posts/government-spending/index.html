Is ageing why government spending keeps rising?

I'd always assumed most of the rise in government spending comes from us getting older. So I checked.

Core Crown spending was 29.9% of GDP in 2000/01 and 32.0% in 2025/26. After inflation, it went from about NZ$17,800 to NZ$27,300 for every person in the country.

Two lines did most of the lifting. NZ Super went from 4.3% to 5.4% of GDP, and health from 5.4% to 7.2%. Together that's 2.9 points, more than the whole 2.1-point rise. Welfare other than Super fell by 1.3 points, which hid some of it.

Super is the clearest case. The number of people receiving it more than doubled, from about 449,000 to 959,000. In 2026 dollars, the yearly bill is up NZ$14.8 billion, and about three-quarters of that is simply more people. The rest is bigger payments per person.

Health is harder. I couldn't find health spending by age, so I can't say how much of that 1.8-point rise is ageing. I'd treat it with care.

The tax side is where ageing bites. In 2000/01 there were 5.5 people aged 15 to 64 for every person aged 65 or over. Now it's 3.7, and Stats NZ's projections have it at 2.9 by 2048. Counting only people in work, it goes from 3.9 to 2.9, and heads for 2.2 if the share of people employed stays where it is.

Workers aren't being asked for much more income tax, though. The average rate on median earnings was 19.0% in 2000/01 and 19.2% in 2025/26. It dipped to 15.1% in 2012, and has crept back up while tax thresholds stayed put for years.

So my first read is partly ageing, and the bigger bill is still ahead. Super and health are the growth lines, and the ratio of workers to retirees keeps falling. But it isn't all demographics. Super payments have risen faster than prices as well, because they follow wages.
